A small trigram language model (trained on public-domain novels) writes text on your CPU. With the watermark on, a secret key nudges each word choice towards a pseudo-random “green list”.

Paste any text. The detector re-computes the green list for every word from the key and the previous word, counts green words and runs a one-proportion z-test. It never needs the language model.

Edit the text the way a user or an attacker might, then re-run detection. Zero-width and homoglyph tricks are undone by normalisation before detection.

C2PA-style provenance: sign a SHA-256 hash of the text with Ed25519, then verify. Change one character and the hard binding breaks. (Teaching format, not a conformant C2PA manifest.)

Research & teaching code. Detection results are statistical evidence about one key, never proof of who wrote a text. · Tutorial
